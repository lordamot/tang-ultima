Case A.  CMD 9 reaches sysctrl.v, the magic byte matches, reconfig pulses
and reconfig_cnt is loaded - and the FPGA does not reload.  Case B is
out: no configuration is attempted at all, so the jump address is not the
fault.  What is left is A1, pin 9 never reaching a low level because
something on the board holds it high (it sits in the corner with TMS 5,
TCK 6, TDI 7, TDO 8 and the oscillator on 4 - board infrastructure, so
the on-board BL616 is the likely holder), or A2, the pin going low and
the configuration controller ignoring it.  Telling them apart needs pin 9
at a header.

Also checked -multiboot_mode and -multiboot_address_width, two options
gowin_tcl.py never set: their values sit beside single|fast|dual|quad (the
jump's SPI read mode) and 24|32 (the address width), so they are not a
trigger switch and the defaults are right.

Loading a core into the FPGA's SRAM is not reachable on this hardware.
It needs a configuration port: JTAG goes to the on-board BL616, not to
the M0S Dock; SSPI needs the MODE straps (pins 87/88, which are the two
buttons here) and its pins are already GPIO in all three cores; and the
card is behind the FPGA, so while the FPGA is unconfigured nothing can
read it, and 907 KB will not fit in the BL616's RAM to buffer.

What does work is the same idea through the flash, and it needs no
RECONFIG_N: an SPI-flash writer in each core with -use_mspi_as_gpio
(MCLK 59, MCS_N 60, MO 61, MI 62, none of them used by the designs),
writing the wanted core into slot 0, then a power cycle - power-up always
loads address 0.  Two sources for the bytes: flash to flash through a
BSRAM buffer, about six seconds, cores staying in the flash; or from .fs
files on the card, tens of seconds, cores becoming files that can be
replaced without a toolchain.  The cost either way is a power cycle per
switch, and a power failure mid-write needs openFPGALoader to recover.

Asked which way to go, and whether pin 9 is reachable on the board -
one multimeter reading would say whether the elegant fix is possible.

Then the Tang Nano 20K schematic v1.3 answered the pin 9 question.  Its
net, PIN09_SYS_~{RECFG}, appears exactly twice in the whole schematic: at
the FPGA's IOR31B/RECONFIG_N, and at TP1.  The six test points are TP1
RECONFIG_N, TP2 GND, TP3 TCK, TP4 TDO, TP5 TDI, TP6 TMS.  The on-board
BL616 is NOT on that net and there is no external pull-up.  So A1 is out
by inspection - 8 mA against 100 uA of internal pull-up and nothing else
must reach 0 V - and it is A2: the pin goes low and the configuration
controller ignores it, -use_reconfign_as_gpio having cut the pad from it.

The fix: leave pin 9 a real RECONFIG_N input (drop
-use_reconfign_as_gpio) and drive TP1 from a free header pin, open-drain,
one wire.  Nothing else changes - the three slots, the ring, mkimage.py
and ultima.c's walk all stay.  Free pins with no configuration function:
25-32 (bank 5) and 48, 49 (bank 3); pin 48 first choice.  The LCD_* nets
do reach the headers - this board's M0S Dock is on pins 41 and 42, whose
nets are PIN41_LCD_R4 and PIN42_LCD_R3.

Built it: all three siblings now place reconfig_n on pin 48 with
OPEN_DRAIN=ON, "RECONFIG_N" : false in each process config so no
-use_reconfign_as_gpio is emitted, and the blink diagnostic reverted.
All three lint clean, all three pass their timing gate with 0 setup and 0
hold violations, each pin report shows reconfig_n at 48/3 IOR49[B] out
with Open Drain ON and pin 9 back to an unassigned RECONFIG_N input, the
ring closes, and the flash was written and read back byte-identical to
bin/ultima.bin.

The board now needs one wire, header pin 48 to TP1, then a power cycle
and F12 -> Core -> Korvet.  Nothing committed.

All three machines in one bitstream is not close: logic 130%, BSRAM 87
blocks of 46, six rPLLs wanted of two at frequencies that cannot be
shared.  Even two do not fit (58 BSRAM of 46).  So reconfiguration is
unavoidable, and without a wire the only lever is what sits at flash
address 0, which power-up always loads.

Built the flash writer.  mister/flashwr.v in all three siblings: a
512-byte buffer and one transaction - shift TX bytes out, read RX back, CS
held - on MCLK 59, MCS_N 60, MO 61, MI 62 at MCLK = clk/4.  The buffer is
what removes the race between the 20 MHz m0s link and the flash clock that
a bit-level bridge would have had.  It is not a flash controller: the whole
W25Q64 command set is in mnano/flashwr.c.  "MSPI" : true in each process
config gives -use_mspi_as_gpio; UG290 4.1.2 table 4-2 says those pins are
configuration pins until DONE and GPIOs after, so a bitstream that takes
them over still boots from the flash it takes over - which is also what
openFPGALoader's own external-flash path does from the JTAG side.

sysctrl CMD 10 passes the payload through.  flash_install() refuses before
it erases - a Winbond JEDEC ID, a file of 64 KB..1 MB, a5 c3 at 0x16,
IDCODE 0000081b at 0x1c - then erases 14 blocks, writes 3545 pages and
reads it all back to compare.  ultima.c lost the ring and the walk;
ultima_switch() installs and returns, and the machine changes on the next
power-up.  The OSD draws Checking/Erasing/Writing/Verifying with a
percentage and "Do not switch off!".  mkimage.py is --fs2bin only, with the
same two checks the firmware makes.  The Makefile has no slots and
bin/ultima.bin is gone; make card says what to copy.

Writing the plan up showed a gap: there was no way to prove the flash bus
works without starting an install that erases address 0.  So flash_probe()
was added - 9F, 05, 03, no WREN, read-only - and ultima_boot() runs it at
every start.  Two console lines then retire the whole risk before anything
is at stake.

Verified here: all three lint; all three build at 0 setup and 0 hold
violations, costing +140/+232/+142 LUTs and one BSRAM block each; the MSPI
pins place on 59/60/61/62 with their configuration functions and pin 9 is a
RECONFIG_N input again; the firmware builds at 461 712 bytes with no
warnings in the new files; make menu-test walks 28 screens with 0 errors.
Nothing has written the flash from user logic - that needs the board.

(hence --skip-reset), and for Gowin that reset is a JTAG RELOAD, which
reconfigures the device from flash address 0 - by then the Korvet.  So the
configuration controller is perfectly willing to reload; it really is only
the RECONFIG_N pad, reused as a GPIO, that loses its path to it.  Anything
holding the FPGA's JTAG can reload the board, which the on-board BL616
does; TP3..TP6 are TCK/TDO/TDI/TMS, so four wires from the companion BL616
would remove the power cycle - against one wire for RECONFIG_N, so the
single wire stays the better option if one is ever wanted.

cut it to about six and is not worth building.

There is no ../tang-mc0511; those two targets are the siblings'.
flash-fpga is `openFPGALoader -b tangnano20k bin/tang.fs` with no -f, and
-m/--write-sram is the default, so it loads the FPGA's SRAM over JTAG -
volatile, gone at power-off.  flash-fpga-flash is `-f -r`, and that -r is
the JTAG RELOAD that booted the Korvet without a power cycle.

The scarce thing is the reconfiguration TRIGGER, not the destination.  An
SRAM load is a reconfiguration driven from outside, and there is no outside
on this board: both openFPGALoader modes are a host talking through the
on-board BL616, which only relays.

Two corrections came out of the digging.  First, my claim that nothing on
the board could hold a bitstream was wrong - the companion BL616 has 4 MB
of its own flash with a 1.44 MB mfg partition, and a bitstream is 907 KB.
Second, the on-board BL616 route is real: it already owns the FPGA's JTAG,
SRAM loading through it is what flash-fpga does, and it is a BL616C with
in-package flash.  It cannot see the card - the schematic says its only
FPGA connections are JTAG 5/6/7/8, UART 69/70 and SPI on 86/76/75/13, no
SDIO - so it would read the bitstream through a running core over that SPI
link and stage it itself.  Zero wires, ~3-8 s, and the FPGA's flash never
written again.

The reason to refuse it: it replaces the firmware of the board's only
programmer.  openFPGALoader stops working unless the new firmware also
emulates the FT2232, and upstream's ft2232d_emulator is not in this tree or
any sibling; a bad flash of that chip leaves nothing able to configure the
FPGA until it is recovered through BOOT.  That trades a board needing a
power cycle for a board that might not be programmable.

Also ruled out: SRAM without persistence, because the goal says the choice
is remembered across power cycles, so any SRAM design must re-apply it at
power-up or write the flash anyway.
