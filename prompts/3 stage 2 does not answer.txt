Picked up from handover.md: the OSD-driven switch (route D through stage 2)
is built and its first test stuck on "Connecting".  Nothing can be run on

- Suspect 2, the BL616 UART pins: CLOSED on paper.  Upstream's own files
  agree with onboard/link.h from two independent directions - MiSTeryNano's
  src/tang/nano20k/atarist.cst puts spi_irqn (an FPGA output, "USB-C UART TX
  output") on pin 69 and uart_rx on 70, and FPGA-Companion's mcu_hw.c for
  TANG_NANO20K has SPI_PIN_IRQ = GPIO 13 "in UART RX, crossed" and
  PIN_UART_TX = GPIO 11.  So 69 -> GPIO 13 (BL616 RX), GPIO 11 -> 70.
  The SDK's bflb_gpio_uart_init routes any BL616 pad through the sig mux
  (sig = pin % 12: 11 and 1, no clash with the console's 21/22 = 9/10),
  and the QFN40 pad check accepts 10..17.  uart1 is in the device table.
- Suspect 5, baud: exact on the BL616 (UART clock XCLK 40 MHz / 20);
  0.012% off on the Korvet (INC 3236 from 40.5 MHz).  Not it.
- Suspect 3, CMD 11: sysctrl.v's gate, first, and data_out tracking are
  the same lines as CMD 10's, in all three cores; coreload.v answers the
  way flashwr.v does; TX/RX are 8N1 LSB-first, idle high.  Nothing found
  by reading - but reading is how the last two designs looked right.
- Upstream never used GPIO 13 as a UART RX (only as a GPIO interrupt) and
  its console on GPIO 11 is the "wo" write-only UART, not uart1; so the
  uart1 routing on those pads is the one thing with no precedent.
